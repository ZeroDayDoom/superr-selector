1. Executive summary

Objective. Identify the T20 batters and bowlers who perform most reliably under pressure, using only the supplied ball-by-ball data.

Dataset. 883 matches and 192,235 deliveries (IPL 2022–2026 and senior T20I competitions, 2022–2026 season labels), after documented exclusions (1,116 players evaluated in total, 38,449 deliveries classified high-pressure, 20% of all balls).

Method. Pressure is a continuous per-delivery index combining a calibrated win-probability model (closeness + leverage) with situational stress (chase steepness, resources, collapse, momentum, phase, match importance). Player value is performance above context-specific expectation models with dynamically-valued wickets. Ratings combine shrunk level, context-adjusted uplift, match consistency, volume and downside risk, with 1,000-draw match-bootstrap uncertainty on every rank.

2. Problem definition

  • Pressure — the pre-delivery match state in which the outcome is in balance and the delivery can materially move it, combined with situational stress (resources, recent collapse, momentum). Defined continuously, not as a fixed rule.
  • Reliability — performance above contextual expectation that repeats across matches and eras, measured with shrinkage and bootstrap intervals.
  • Batting pressure — batting in states where the win probability is contested and resources are scarce: steep chases, collapses, death overs of close matches.
  • Bowling pressure — suppressing expected outcomes when the batting side holds leverage (defending knife-edge totals, containing set batters, taking wickets in high-closeness states).

4. Pressure definition

Pressure score (Model C, primary): P = 0.70·B + 0.20·A + 0.10·R where

  • Model B = 0.45·Closeness + 0.35·Leverage + 0.20·Difficulty
  • Model A (transparent anchor) = 0.30·Difficulty + 0.15·Resource + 0.20·Collapse + 0.15·Momentum + 0.10·Phase + 0.10·Importance
  • Closeness = 1 − |2·WP − 1| (WP = calibrated win probability)
  • Leverage = clip(E|ΔWP| / 0.12, 0, 1) — expected absolute WP swing over the outcome distribution {dot, 1, 2-3, 4, 6, wicket, extras}, computed from hypothetical post-outcome states (never the realised outcome)
  • Difficulty (chase) = 0.45·clip(RRR/12) + 0.35·clip((RRR−CRR)/6) + 0.20·clip(boundary burden); (first innings) score-vs-par and rate-vs-par terms
  • Resource stress R = 1 − (balls fraction × wickets fraction)
  • Collapse = 0.6·min(1, wk12/3) + 0.4·min(1, wk6/2), Momentum = 0.5·clip(dots/12) + 0.5·clip(boundary drought/18), Phase = 0.3/0.4/1.0 (PP/middle/death), Importance = knockout 1.0 / league 0.2

Weights were calibrated on the 2025 validation season against a pre-registered criterion (outcome-variance ratio, realized-|WPA| ratio, split-half stability); thresholds are quantiles (top 10–30% grid).

Evidence that HP states differ. High-pressure deliveries (top 20% within innings × phase) show a wicket probability of 629.7% vs 518.3% in ordinary states, a mean realised |WPA| of 2.31× the ordinary rate, and higher economy (8.75 vs 8.74).

5. Feature engineering

128 documented features (full dictionary in reports/feature_dictionary.md), leakage-audited by outcome perturbation (0 violations). Major groups:

  • Match state: score/wickets/balls-before, chase state (target, RRR, gap), DLS-aware, resources remaining
  • Par/expected state: leave-one-innings-out expected score, runs remaining, venue-season par (shrinkage 8, leave-one-match-out)
  • Momentum: rolling 6/12/18/24 windows (runs, wickets, dots, boundaries), dot streak, boundary drought
  • Collapse: wickets in recent windows, cluster severity
  • Batting responsibility: balls faced, setness, entry state, career dismissal risk (prior-matches-only)
  • Bowler state: spell, recent economy/dots/wickets, boundary streak conceded, team wicket drought
  • Role & matchup: data-driven batter/bowler roles, handedness × bowler-class league rates (leave-one-season-out, EB-shrunk)
  • Wicket drought / new batter / set batter: explicit flags and severity

6. Model architecture

  • Win probability: calibrated logistic regression (selected over HistGradientBoosting and LightGBM on the 2026 holdout: AUC 0.915 vs 0.836/0.845); isotonic calibration on 2025. First-innings WP uses a total-projection framing (AUC 0.657 — a documented weak point).
  • Expected outcomes: LightGBM multiclass over mutually exclusive buckets (dot/single/2-3/four/six/wicket/other for batting; bowler-wicket attribution split for bowling); expected runs, dot, boundary, wicket and conceded-runs metrics are consistent by construction.
  • Deep learning: multi-task MLP (256-128-64, embeddings, CE + BCE heads) and GRU sequence model (previous 12 deliveries) trained and evaluated under the identical protocol; retained as experiments unless they beat the tabular winner (see reports/model_training_report.md).
  • Validation: chronological splits (train ≤2024, validate 2025, holdout 2026); never random ball splits.

7. Player performance metrics

  • Batting value: BV = (runs − xRuns) − λ(state)·(striker dismissed − xWicket) where λ(state) is the dynamic wicket value — expected remaining runs lost by moving from w to w+1 wickets down within the same balls-left bin (mean λ ≈ 9.83 runs; a wicket costs most mid-innings with resources available and least at the death).
  • Bowling value: BoV = (xConceded − conceded) + λ(state)·(bowler wicket − xW); run-outs/retired-out/obstructing never credited.
  • WPA: realised win-probability added (bowling sign-flipped).
  • Pressure uplift: HP mean − normal mean, and the context-adjusted residual of HP mean on normal mean (the ranking uses the residual so a poor normal baseline is not rewarded).
  • Consistency: positive-HP-match share, match-level sd (match level, never delivery level). Downside: CVaR5 of HP-match means.
  • Reliability: HP balls/matches/seasons/scenario diversity + per-player match-cluster bootstrap SE.

8. Sample size and eligibility

Tiers derived from the HP-balls distribution: inclusive ≥40 balls & ≥6 matches; moderate ≥60 & ≥8 (headline lists); strict ≥100 & ≥12. Every player appears in outputs/eligibility_audit.csv with reasons. Shrinkage: empirical-Bayes method-of-moments (k estimated from between/within variance; k/2 and 2k sensitivities stored). All rankings carry 1,000-draw bootstrap rank intervals.

9. Fair comparisons

  • Roles are data-driven (actual batting positions, overs-bowled phase shares); expectation models are conditioned on phase and role; a role-normalised ranking variant is reported (Section 14).
  • IPL vs T20I: competition fixed effects in the expectation models; the ranking is recomputed on each competition separately (Section 14).
  • Opposition strength: two-pass quality terms in the expectation models (no country lists; A-team fixtures excluded).
  • Venue/era: shrinkage-adjusted venue-season par.
  • Opportunity: scenario diversity, seasons and opponents tracked per player and fed into the volume term.

10. Validation (actual results)

  • Temporal persistence (HP value in early eras vs later eras): rank_2022-2023_eval_2024-2026: Spearman 0.038; rank_2022-2024_eval_2025-2026: Spearman 0.032; rank_2022-2025_eval_2026-2026: Spearman 0.212.
  • Split-half (batters): Spearman 0.093, Kendall 0.064, mean top-10 overlap 0.8/10 over 100 splits.
  • Win probability: holdout AUC 0.915, Brier 0.127, calibration slope 0.99; ECE ≤ 0.04 within every pressure quintile.
  • Model comparison (reports/model_comparison.csv): LightGBM won expected-runs tasks on validation log loss; league baseline and logistic baselines documented alongside.
  • Bootstrap: 1,000 match-level draws; rank P05/P50/P95 and P(top-10) per player (outputs/top10_probability.csv).
  • Sensitivity: thresholds (top 10–30%), pressure models (A/B/C), competition scopes, eligibility tiers, shrinkage strengths, composite weight variants — all rankings and Spearman stabilities stored in outputs/rank_stability.csv and summarised in Section 14.
  • Ablation: removing each composite component one at a time (Section 14 table).

15. Limitations

Data: no ball-tracking, fielding positions, pitch reports, weather, dew, boundary dimensions, injury or team-strategy information; bowl_speed mostly unrecorded; venue identifiers without names. Model: innings-1 win probability is a projection proxy; opponent adjustment is two-pass rather than full hierarchical; clutch effects in sport are small, so uplift estimates are shrunk and interval-quantified, not claimed as certainties. Sample: 2026 seasons may post-date some T20I cycles unevenly; role changes across a career are captured only at innings granularity. Psychology is unobserved — the index measures situational performance, not mental traits.

16. Conclusion

The most reliable players under pressure are those who combine a high shrunk level on HP deliveries, positive context-adjusted uplift, match-to- match consistency and controlled downside, with enough HP volume that the bootstrap intervals are tight. The final lists above are the composite ranking under the primary specification; the multiverse in Section 14 shows which selections are robust and which are fragile. Main analytical insight: pressure performance is measurable but modest in persistence — level and reliability carry the ranking, uplift confirms it, and the bootstrap disciplines it.
